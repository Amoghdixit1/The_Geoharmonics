      How Earth Jukebox works

      1. Real NASA frames. Each record is a visualization from NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio used in the Earth Information Center. The video plays in your browser and every musical step reads the frame currently on screen.
      2. Pixels back to data. We sampled each visualization's own colour bar. A pixel's colour is matched to the nearest colour on that bar, giving back the value it stands for — °C, ppm, metres. For shaded imagery we compare chroma (colour without brightness), so shadows don't fool it.
      3. Where on Earth. Most records are flat (equirectangular) maps, so each pixel has a latitude and longitude. That powers place names in descriptions and the stereo position of sounds.
      4. The scanner. Every bar, a playhead sweeps from west to east. Step 1 is the far west (the Pacific date line), step 16 the far east. Time in the bar and left–right position both mean longitude, on every record.
      5. Musical rules. Pitches are snapped to a scale chosen per record, so data never clashes. A voice limit, compressor and limiter keep busy data from becoming noise.
      6. Every sound has a meaning. The mixer lists each instrument and what it represents. Solo one to listen to a single variable.

      Data credits: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio and the science teams named on each record. This project is not endorsed by NASA.
